The big idea

Every choice costs something

Real mission planners never get everything they want. Neither will you. Here are the five things you'll juggle, and the real trade-off behind each one.

🫁

Life support

Each person breathes about 0.84 kg of oxygen a day. Making air takes power, so every watt spent on science is air you didn't make.

⚡

Power

Mars gets only 43% of Earth's sunlight, and a dust storm can cut it to almost nothing. You decide what stays on tonight.

🛡️

Radiation

Building shielding protects you forever, but it costs tonight's battery. Sheltering from a storm is safe, but it stops all work.

🌱

Food

Plants need power every day, and food you plant today won't be ready for days. Save power now, go hungry later.

📡

Distance

Earth can be 20+ light-minutes away. Sometimes asking for help is smart. In an emergency, the answer comes too late.

How a mission plays

Read. Decide. Live with it.

  1. 1

    Something happens

    A leak, a solar flare, a dust storm, a strange rock. Each chapter starts with a moment that needs a leader.

  2. 2

    Check your meters

    Air, power and radiation tell you what you can afford. Every option shows its cost before you pick it.

  3. 3

    Open the Rulebook… or don't

    Ask Earth for advice if there's time, or follow the Commander's rules. Or trust your gut.

  4. 4

    See what happens

    Your choices follow you. A shortcut in Chapter 1 can catch up with you in Chapter 5.

Commander's Rulebook

  1. Air first, then heat, then talk, then food, then science.
  2. Emergency? Checklist first, then tell Earth.
  3. Radiation warning means shelter. No exceptions.
  4. Never go outside alone.
  5. Keep a reserve: never below 30%.
  1. Charge up before the dark.
  2. Check in with Earth every sol.
  3. Quarantine anything sick.
  4. When there's time, ask.
  5. Science never beats safety.

The Rulebook was written the hard way

Every rule comes from something real: the buddy system on NASA spacewalks, the storm shelters on the Space Station, and the dust storm that ended the Opportunity rover in 2018.

Following the rules isn't always the exciting choice. Sometimes it means losing a whole day, or watching a discovery sit just out of reach. But only players who follow the book and manage their meters well reach the two best endings.

Questions

Is the data really from NASA?

Yes. Solar flares and radiation storms come from NASA's DONKI database (live when you're online, from a saved archive when you're not). The game always shows which one you're seeing. Mars weather, radiation and biology facts come from NASA missions and the Open Science Data Repository.

Can my child lose?

Yes, and that's part of the learning. Missions can end early, but nobody dies. The crew is rescued, and the ending explains which choice caused it so kids can try again.

Do we need to install anything?

No. AeroPlay runs in the browser. There's no account and no personal data collected.

Is AeroPlay made by NASA?

No. AeroPlay is an independent project that uses NASA's free, public open data. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by NASA.
